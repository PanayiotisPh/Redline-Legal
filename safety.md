# Safety notice

**Redline is not a driving aid. It is a record of driving you have already
done.**

## Before you drive

Start the trip, mount the phone, then set off. Everything the app records
happens without you touching it.

## While you drive

**Do not touch your phone.** No statistic in this app is worth looking away
from the road for. Distance, speed and G-force are all still there when you
stop.

If you use Android Auto, the car display is deliberately minimal and updates
about once a second — slowly enough to glance at, not to read. It exists so
you are less tempted to reach for your phone, not to give you something to
watch.

## Performance features

The sprint timer and segment timing measure hard acceleration.

**Use them on private land, a closed circuit, or a legal test venue.** Do not
use them to time yourself on a public road. Speed limits apply regardless of
what any app is measuring, and a timed run on a public road endangers other
people.

Redline has no global leaderboard, deliberately. You are never competing with
strangers, so there is never a reason to drive harder than is safe in order
to move up a list.

## Mounting your phone

Use a proper mount. A phone loose in a cup holder or on a seat produces poor
readings — and becomes a projectile in a collision.

Keep the mount clear of airbag deployment zones and out of your view of the
road. In many countries an obstructed windscreen is itself an offence.

## Battery and heat

Recording uses GPS and sensors continuously and will drain the battery faster
than normal. On a long drive, use a charger. Phones in direct sunlight on a
windscreen mount can overheat and throttle or shut down.

## If something looks wrong

Sensors can be wrong. If a figure looks implausible, it probably is — a bad
GPS fix, a knocked mount, or a phone that was not still when the trip
started. Do not act on a number you do not believe.
